## A. Claim-atomicity and unit-symbol binding corrections (Batch 04)

**Finding:** the inductive-reactance and capacitive-reactance `UNIT_SYMBOL` rows each bundled two clauses under one `sourceId` whose own bound passage supported only the first -- e.g. the inductive-reactance row cited `SRC-BIPM-BROCHURE` (which states only "inductance | henry | H") for an added "inductive reactance ... is measured in ohms" clause BIPM's own passage never states. The impedance `UNIT_SYMBOL` row rested on a similar inference from the same BIPM passage, which establishes the ohm for resistance only, not impedance. Separately, the capacitance `DEFINITION` claim asserted capacitance is a "fixed" ability to store charge -- a word never used in the cited source's actual retrieved passage -- and the capacitive-reactance `DISTINCTION` claim carried a fabricated parenthetical annotation that was never actual page text, an editorial cross-reference wrongly embedded inside a quoted-passage field.

**Correction:** each defect split into atomic, single-clause claims, each bound only to the source that actually supports it. The ohm claim for both reactance rows and for impedance is now bound to a newly-registered source, `SRC-IEC-IEV-131-12-43` "impedance" (IEC 60050 Electropedia), whose own passage states `Z = R + jX` and "the coherent SI unit of impedance is ohm, Ω" -- since resistance and reactance are added together to form impedance, they necessarily share its unit; this is a direct textual entailment, not an inference from an unrelated quantity's silence. The unsupported "fixed" word was removed from the capacitance definition; the fabricated annotation was removed from the capacitive-reactance distinction claim, which was rebuilt as two genuinely, independently-sourced atomic claims. **No evidence-status changed as a result of this section -- every affected row was, and remains, VERIFIED.** This is a claim-atomicity and source-binding correction, not a re-adjudication.

See `UNIT202-CORRECTION-AMENDMENT-LEDGER.json` entries tagged `SOURCE_PROVENANCE_CORRECTION` / `CLAIM_ATOMICITY_CORRECTION` (batch-04) for the full before/after record.

## B. Structural claim-dimension coverage and the normalizedClaimRefs cache

**Finding:** two related structural gaps. (1) A `normalizedClaim`'s relationship to its row's `requiredCoverageDimensions` was implicit, inferable only from prose -- a VERIFIED status could rest on "some permitted claim exists somewhere on this row" without any structural guarantee that a *specific* required dimension was the one actually supported. (2) Every Batches 04-06 learning point's `normalizedClaimRefs` field was a hand-maintained, inconsistently-truncated preview (commonly cut to ~100 characters mid-sentence) of its underlying evidence rows' claims, with no mechanical check that it stayed in sync with those rows.

**Correction:** every `normalizedClaim` across Batches 04-06 now carries an explicit `dimension` array naming which of its row's own `requiredCoverageDimensions` it supports. Validator check 51 is strengthened to require every required dimension to have at least one permitted, registered, *correctly-tagged* claim, and additionally requires every claim's declared dimension(s) to be a genuine subset of its own row's required dimensions (no claim may over-claim a dimension outside its row's scope). Separately, every learning point's `normalizedClaimRefs` was mechanically rebuilt as an exact, exhaustive, current cache: for every `evidenceRequirementId` an LP references, every one of that row's `normalizedClaims` is rendered as `${sourceId}: ${claimText}` (truncated only past 140 characters), in the evidence row's own order. New validator check 58 mechanically re-derives the expected cache from the live evidence rows and fails on any mismatch, so this cannot silently drift again.

**Section D disclosure (the scope boundary check 51's own code comment refers to):** check 51 mechanically verifies STRUCTURAL claim-dimension coverage only -- every required dimension has at least one permitted, registered, correctly-tagged claim, and every claim's declared dimension(s) are a real subset of its row's own required dimensions. It does **not**, and cannot, verify that a compound claim's cited passage genuinely supports every clause the claim text asserts -- that is a reading-comprehension judgment, made by hand during this pass's own review (Section A above documents the specific compound-claim defects found and fixed this way for Batch 04's reactance/impedance rows). A PASS on check 51 is a structural-coverage guarantee, not a semantic-correctness guarantee; Sections A, E and F of this report document the semantic (does-the-passage-really-say-that) review performed by hand for the highest-stakes corrections in this pass.

## E. EMI-LP-16 (Fleming's right-hand rule) -- RESOLVED

The second pass confirmed a genuine citation trail (Wikipedia → Hughes, *Electrical & Electronic Technology*, 12th ed., p.145) but could not render or read that edition's page through any available tool, after ten independent access attempts, and left the requirement genuinely held.

**This pass located and directly read the equivalent passage in a different, earlier edition of the same published textbook**: Edward Hughes (revised by John Hiley, Keith Brown and Ian McKenzie Smith), *Hughes Electrical and Electronic Technology*, 10th edition, Pearson Education Limited, 2008, ISBN 978-0-13-206011-0, Chapter 6 "Electromagnetism", section 6.9(a) "Fleming's right-hand rule", printed page 141 -- independently confirmed genuine by its own header, page number and section sequence (immediately followed by section 6.9(b) "Lenz's law"). The passage states in text: "If the first finger of the right hand is pointed in the direction of the magnetic flux ... and if the thumb is pointed in the direction of motion of the conductor relative to the magnetic field, then the second finger, held at right angles to both the thumb and the first finger, represents the direction of the e.m.f."

Retrieved via a FlipHTML5-hosted page-image scan. **FlipHTML5 is disclosed only as the retrieval route, in every field** (`sourceRef`, `sourceLocator`, `SOURCE-REGISTER.json` publisher) -- the cited authority throughout is the published Hughes textbook itself, never the hosting site. EMI-LP-16 → **READY**. This closes the last core (non-context) blocker in Batch 05.
