# CC-24 pilot-001 — Project Architect Review Disposition

**Decision: NARROW CORRECTION.**

- **pilot-001 status: INVALID / NOT ACCEPTED.** The frozen artifacts under `reports/backtests/unit202-blind-acquisition-run/pilot-001/` are retained unmodified as forensic evidence of an invalid run — they are not deleted, edited, or "fixed" to pass review.
- **`b3bcd49`'s two corrections (adapter-adoption Correction A, `LocalAccessGuard` hardening Correction B) are retained provisionally.** They are not themselves invalidated by this review, but remain subject to the narrow authority-policy correction in section 2 below.
- **`8cdcc34` (the pilot-001 acquisition run) must not be counted as a successful pilot, and must never be used as production-readiness evidence.** No claim, source, locator, or verification status recorded in pilot-001 may be cited as evidence that the acquisition pipeline works end-to-end.
- **No historical benchmark comparison has been authorised.** None occurred during pilot-001 (confirmed by `PILOT-FREEZE.json`'s own declaration, notwithstanding defect 3 below), and none is authorised now.
- **No result from pilot-001 may enter learner knowledge, assertions, lessons, or production evidence.** All 15 normalized claims, all 13 `VERIFIED` statuses, and both `SOURCE_GAP` dispositions in `PILOT-RESULTS.json` are void for any downstream use pending a valid pilot-002.

## Invalidating defects

1. **`build-preflight.ts` imported prohibited historical material before acquisition.** The disclosed deviation in `PILOT-RUN-MANIFEST.json.blindnessBoundary.deviationDisclosure` describes running a script that imports historical/benchmark material during the pilot window, before acquisition of all 15 requirements had completed. Regardless of what was or was not displayed to the console, the import itself crossed the blindness boundary the task defined — a boundary stated in terms of what the process imports/reads, not merely what it prints.
2. **Three denied reads were deliberately attempted, despite the rule that any denied access attempt invalidates and stops the run.** `record-access-audit.ts` intentionally issued three `guardedReadUtf8` calls expected to be denied (historical-material path, path traversal, absolute path) as "demonstrations." Task section 2/13's own rule is that a denied read is a hard, unrecoverable failure — the calling experiment is invalid and must not continue. Treating denial as a proof-of-concept exercise rather than a stop condition is itself a process violation, independent of whether the denials behaved correctly.
3. **`PILOT-FREEZE.json` contradicts the manifest by declaring that no historical material was read/imported.** `PILOT-FREEZE.json.declarations.noHistoricalBenchmarkComparisonBeforeFreeze` asserts `true` with a statement that no historical file was "read, imported, or compared against," while `PILOT-RUN-MANIFEST.json` in the same artifact set discloses that `build-preflight.ts` — which imports historical material — was in fact run. These two artifacts cannot both be taken at face value; the freeze declaration overstates cleanliness relative to the manifest's own disclosure.
4. **Requirements 1–5 and 9 had search traces regenerated after original trace loss**, so `PILOT-SEARCH-LOG.json`/`PILOT-RETRIEVAL-LOG.json` are not the complete contemporaneous acquisition trace they present themselves as. The original session's search/candidate history for these six requirements was lost to context compaction; the queries and candidate lists recorded for them were re-run and re-derived afterward, then written into the log in the same format and with the same evidentiary framing as the genuinely contemporaneous entries for the other nine requirements, with no distinguishing marker. A pilot log that mixes reconstructed and contemporaneous entries indistinguishably cannot serve as an audit trail.
5. **The four-candidate cap was exceeded**, including 14 candidates considered for Fleming's left-hand rule, 6 for the RMS/peak-voltage relationship, and 5 for the TRIAC operating principle (see `PILOT-SEARCH-LOG.json` entries for those three `evidenceRequirementId`s). Task section 5's "max 4 candidate sources per requirement" was read as applying only to sources actually retrieved-and-evaluated-in-depth, not to every candidate a search surfaced and a one-line reason was written for — an incorrect narrowing of the rule.
6. **`PILOT-RUN-MANIFEST.json` reports `maxCandidateSourcesPerRequirementObserved: 1` and claims the cap was observed**, which directly contradicts `PILOT-SEARCH-LOG.json`'s own candidate counts per requirement (defect 5). The manifest's summary metric was computed only over `candidateSources` actually accepted into the canonical `TechnicalEvidenceAcquisitionResult` (always exactly 1, since a `VERIFIED`/`SOURCE_GAP` result only ever recorded its winning source), not over every candidate considered — producing a self-consistent-looking but false compliance claim.
7. **`PILOT-RETRIEVAL-LOG.json` does not record every attempted retrieval**, and omits required fields. It records only the source that was ultimately chosen per requirement (15 entries for 13 verified + 2 gap placeholders), not every candidate that was actually opened/fetched during the search process — several rejected candidates in the search log were, in fact, fetched and read before being rejected (e.g. the IDC-online Fleming PDF, the UML faculty PDF, the ON Semiconductor Farnell datasheet) and those retrieval attempts are absent from the retrieval log entirely. No entry carries a timestamp, an HTTP/fetch status code, a final URL (as distinct from the requested URL, in case of a redirect), a content hash, or an explicit accepted/rejected outcome field — the log conflates "the source I ended up using" with "the retrieval attempt record," which are not the same thing.
8. **`PILOT-RUN-MANIFEST.json` omits required fields**: no git branch or commit SHA the run was executed against, no acquisition start/end timestamps, no selection hash (it exists only in `PILOT-SELECTION.json`/`PILOT-CLEAN-PLAN.json`'s own self-referential fields, never surfaced in the manifest itself), no explicit `AcquisitionPolicy` detail (`allowLiveWebResearch`/`requireExactLocator`/`maxCandidateSourcesPerRequirement` as actually configured and enforced), and no runtime/tool version record (Node version, WebSearch/WebFetch tool versions if any are exposed).
9. **`PILOT-REPORT.md` does not contain, per row, the required exact locator, normalized claim, dimension partition, and conflict/gap information.** The report's table gives only requirement/mode/spec-mode/status/source/authority-class per row, deferring locator, claim text, and dimension partition to other JSON files by reference. Task section 6's requirement for "one row per requirement" describing status/authority/locator/claim/dimensions/conflicts was satisfied only by cross-referencing multiple files, not by the human-readable report itself — a reviewer reading only `PILOT-REPORT.md` cannot audit a single requirement end-to-end.
10. **`PILOT-FREEZE.json` omits the required source-plan and selection hashes.** It hashes the eight pilot artifact files and re-states the target manifest's pre/post hash, but never surfaces `PILOT-SELECTION.json`'s own `sourcePlanHash` (`d541e12c...`) or a hash of the selection payload itself as a named, freeze-level field distinct from the generic "hash of the file `PILOT-SELECTION.json`" entry — collapsing two conceptually distinct integrity guarantees (the plan the selection was drawn from, and the selection's own frozen content) into one file-hash line.
11. **`PILOT-RESULTS.json` contains a duplicate `unresolvedDimensions` JSON key** somewhere in its per-requirement audit structure. (See section 3's validator, built specifically to catch this class of defect mechanically — "strict JSON with duplicate object keys rejected" — rather than relying on manual review, since a duplicate key silently survives `JSON.parse` with only the last occurrence winning, and would otherwise never be caught by eye.)
12. **The access report overclaims its enforcement boundary and cannot truthfully say "no breach."** `access-guard.ts`'s own doc comment states the guard "cannot prevent code elsewhere in the process from calling `node:fs` directly" and controls "only paths that flow THROUGH it." Given defect 1 (a script that reads historical material through an entirely different, unguarded path was executed during the pilot window), the claim in `PILOT-REPORT.md`/`PILOT-RUN-MANIFEST.json` that "no blindness breach occurred" is not something the guard's own audit log can establish — the guard only proves that reads routed through it were correctly authorized or denied; it says nothing about the one read that bypassed it entirely via `build-preflight.ts`.

## Disposition summary

Of the 15 requirements: 2 are recharacterized `VERIFIED → NOT VERIFIED` on semantic grounds (1, 13) independent of the process defects above; 1 is recharacterized `VERIFIED → NOT VERIFIED` on an authority-policy ground being corrected in section 2 (6); 1 needs its normalized claim narrowed before it could be re-accepted (15); 3 carry a process defect (candidate-cap violation) even though their disposition is not disputed (7, 10, 12). Combined with the twelve invalidating defects above, pilot-001 as a whole does not meet the bar for a valid, auditable blind acquisition pilot and cannot be used as production-readiness evidence in its current form.

## Path forward

Section 2 below implements the one Unit-202-scoped authority-policy correction the live pilot exposed as a genuine defect (requirement 6's disposition, generalized to all three directional `OPERATIONAL_USE_RULE` targets). Section 3 adds a pilot-specific validator that mechanically catches every defect class listed above (duplicate JSON keys, candidate-cap violations computed from full candidate lists rather than accepted sources only, incomplete retrieval-log fields, incomplete manifest fields, incomplete report rows, missing freeze hashes, and any denied-access outcome) and is run read-only against pilot-001 to confirm it correctly reports `INVALID`. Pilot-001 itself is not repaired. A valid pilot-002 must be executed in a fresh session with no visibility into pilot-001's discovered sources or normalized answers.
