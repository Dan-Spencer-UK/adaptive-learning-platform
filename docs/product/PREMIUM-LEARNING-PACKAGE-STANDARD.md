# Premium Learning Package Standard

## Product promise

ALP is intended to be a class-leading vocational learning product. Premium visuals, clear teaching, rigorous assessment and useful adaptation are core product features, not polish layers.

For V1, useful adaptation means **assessment-driven Guided Revision**, not dynamically changing every ordinary lesson.

## A learner-ready lesson should feel authored, not assembled

The learner should experience:
- a clear reason for the lesson;
- one coherent canonical route through the lesson;
- substantial explanation rather than disconnected one-sentence cards;
- scrolling where it preserves a stronger teaching experience;
- visuals whenever they materially improve understanding;
- more than token imagery where reinforcement genuinely benefits from another representation;
- high-quality physical/symbol recognition where relevant;
- worked examples where useful;
- short embedded checks that do not reveal their own answers;
- feedback written for a learner rather than an engineer;
- concise completion without duplicated summary screens.

A lesson is not a deck of viewport-sized slides. A teaching section may extend below the fold when the material belongs together.

The preferred pattern is:

> **chunked explanation + excellent visuals + reinforcement + examples/interactions + concise check**

## Visual ambition

A premium ALP lesson must not default to text just because text is easier to generate.

For conceptual, spatial, physical, directional and component-recognition topics, visual instruction is expected unless there is a documented reason not to use it.

Visuals should be:
- technically exact where required;
- original ALP artwork;
- cohesive with the product-wide design system;
- legible on a phone;
- pedagogically purposeful;
- strong enough to carry part of the explanation rather than merely decorate it;
- available in assessment-safe form where needed.

V1 visual effort should prioritise exceptional canonical lesson assets and assessment-safe variants. Bespoke misconception/remediation visual branches are post-V1 unless separately approved.

## Content ambition

- course/syllabus fidelity is mandatory;
- no unrelated "interesting" content drift;
- questions do not test untaught content;
- natural learner-centred wording;
- coherent sections rather than arbitrary Continue fragmentation;
- enough explanation/reinforcement to teach, not merely state, the concept;
- formula/symbol notation matches the course and product conventions;
- diagnostic complexity only where it creates real learner value.

## V1 adaptive product promise

V1 should be simple for the learner to understand:

> **Learn → take a formative/mock assessment → submit → see what to revise → use Guided Revision → reassess → receive an updated plan.**

Guided Revision:
- is generated only from a completed and submitted formative/mock assessment;
- ranks the full canonical lessons needed, highest weakness first;
- reuses the same premium lessons as normal Learn;
- is rebuilt after the next completed/submitted assessment;
- is not silently changed by ordinary lesson checks or by merely opening a revision lesson.

## Quality threshold

A lesson that is technically correct but visually weak, under-explained, awkwardly structured or pedagogically confusing is not learner-ready.

Likewise, an assessment that produces a score but cannot map demonstrated weaknesses to useful canonical lessons is not sufficient for the V1 adaptive proposition.
