| 2026-08-21 | Knowledge coverage is semantic, not merely referential: a curriculum requirement (Assessment Criterion or Range item) is knowledge-complete only when its governed assertions collectively satisfy every discrete factual/procedural obligation the requirement imposes -- never merely because at least one assertion, or N assertions, or one assertion per Range item, happens to be mapped to it. Referential coverage (something is mapped) and semantic knowledge completeness (enough is mapped) must always be reported and gated as separate dimensions. A new pre-authoring lifecycle stage, KNOWLEDGE-OBLIGATION DECOMPOSITION, decomposes each requirement's discrete obligations before mass assertion authoring -- an authoring/audit artifact, never learner content, and deliberately a plain data file rather than a new governed database entity. | CC-09B.1. Directly motivated by an independent Project Architect audit finding CC-09B's coverage checks were referential-only ("false-green": one lever assertion had wrongly marked a "levers, gears and pulleys" requirement complete) and had also under-reported syllabus-only technical provenance (~32 assertions found citing City & Guilds as their only factual source, against zero previously reported). See `PROJECT-STATUS.md` §CC-09B.1 for the full correction, the hardened `report-coverage-matrix.ts` three-dimension model (curriculum structure / referential coverage / semantic knowledge completeness, plus a separate direct-factual-provenance audit), and the two regression tests proving both failure modes are now structurally caught. | 2026-08-21 | Source-first evidence authoring: no externally grounded factual assertion may be authored or materially rewritten until the actual source passage has been identified and inspected, the proposition(s) it actually supports determined, and the assertion authored to stay within that boundary -- never the reverse ("write a plausible assertion, then find a citation"). A source being real, authoritative and on-topic does not by itself entail the specific proposition attached to it; an application/construction/industry-practice claim never inherits factual support solely from a generic component-property assertion. `DERIVED_FROM` may substitute for an assertion's own direct provenance only when the child is a deductive MATHEMATICAL or LOGICAL_DEFINITIONAL consequence of its parent(s), never when it introduces new EMPIRICAL/APPLICATION content -- the content-schema now mechanically requires every `DERIVED_FROM` edge to declare which kind it is. A cited locator's support for an assertion's whole proposition (DIRECT) versus only part of it (PARTIAL) is now an explicit, optional classification on provenance links, reused rather than duplicated into a parallel evidence database. | CC-09B.2. Directly motivated by an independent Project Architect audit finding that CC-09B.1's stronger provenance/semantic-completeness checks still let "reference-shaped decoration" through: a source could be real and on-topic while not actually entailing the assertion it was attached to (e.g. "a photodiode responds to light" does not entail "photodiodes are used in security-alarm systems"). All 49 pre-existing `DERIVED_FROM` relationships were audited with no sampling; 15 across 8 declarations were found to be exactly this EMPIRICAL_APPLICATION pattern (wattmeter, energy meter, multimeter, continuity test, heating/boiler-control, security-alarm, telephone, wireless-control) and corrected -- six with genuine newly inspected direct evidence, two (telephone, wireless-control) narrowed and honestly marked incomplete in `unit202-knowledge-obligations.ts` rather than force-citing a weak source, deliberately flipping AC6.1 from CC-09B.1's 23/23 to 22/23 semantically complete. See `PROJECT-STATUS.md` §CC-09B.2 for the full correction and the four regression tests (task section 36) proving both the application-inference and device-construction-inference failure modes are now structurally caught, and that valid mathematical derivations and partial multi-source support are still correctly recognised. | 2026-08-21 | Source specificity must never become or exceed syllabus scope: a technical source may (and often does) document far more detail than a Level 2 qualification requires, but only the proportionate governed proposition -- required or reasonably necessary at the AC/Range item's own stated cognitive verb and depth -- may become required curriculum knowledge. A named Range item is a boundary on what may be examined, never an invitation to expand into everything a cited source happens to also cover. A specific product/manufacturer/protocol/terminal-naming example may remain in provenance/evidence metadata as illustrative support without itself becoming mandatory learner-facing content, a mastery-gate dependency, or a question-blueprint recall target.
