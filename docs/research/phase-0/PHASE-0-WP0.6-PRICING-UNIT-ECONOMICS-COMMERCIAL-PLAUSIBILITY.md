# Phase 0 --- WP0.6: Pricing, Unit Economics and Commercial Plausibility Model

**Status:** Draft for Product Owner review\
**Date:** 13 August 2026\
**Market:** United Kingdom first\
**Depends on:** WP0.1--WP0.5\
**Purpose:** Determine whether the enterprise remains commercially
plausible after realistic pricing, VAT, payment costs, infrastructure,
AI usage, customer acquisition, content-production and operating-cost
assumptions are applied.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Executive conclusion

## Preliminary commercial verdict: GO, under an ultra-lean bootstrap strategy

The enterprise should **not** be financed or modelled initially as a
conventionally staffed SaaS company.

The intended financing strategy is:

> **Pre-revenue: minimise incremental cash expenditure. Founder + AI
> perform development, initial content work, research, outreach and
> product operation.**

> **Post-revenue: up to 100% of revenue may be reinvested where
> expenditure improves the learner product, reduces material risk,
> increases development/content throughput, or produces demonstrated
> favourable growth.**

> **Do not assume external funding is required. Revenue should finance
> professionalisation and expansion wherever practicable.**

The first financial question is therefore not:

> "Can the business immediately support £130,000--£300,000 of annual
> company costs?"

It is:

> **"Can the Product Owner reach paying users for very little
> incremental cash and then make the product cash-self-sustaining?"**

The preliminary answer is **yes, plausibly**.

A second model must nevertheless remain alongside the bootstrap cash
model:

> **Would the enterprise still have attractive economics later if
> founder labour, specialist content work, engineering, support,
> security and other functions had to be paid at commercial rates?**

This prevents us from proving only that the project works because the
founder works for free.

The two financial views are therefore:

``` text
BOOTSTRAP CASH MODEL
Can the founder build → launch → earn revenue → reinvest → grow
without substantial pre-revenue capital?

                     +

MATURE ECONOMIC MODEL
Can the resulting company eventually pay people properly,
operate professionally and still produce attractive margins?
```

Both must remain viable.

The principal bootstrap risks are not hosting costs. They are:

1.  whether learners will pay for a product when basic revision
    questions are available free;
2.  whether differentiated diagnosis/remediation genuinely improves the
    learner experience;
3.  whether governed content can be produced efficiently by the
    founder + AI workflow;
4.  whether organic/outreach distribution can generate the first
    meaningful cohort;
5.  whether later professionalisation can be financed from revenue;
6.  whether the second vertical genuinely benefits from reuse.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1.1 Bootstrap financing principle

The project should adopt the following default rule:

> **Spend as little as reasonably possible before revenue. Once revenue
> exists, reinvest aggressively but selectively.**

The Product Owner is willing to reinvest **up to every penny of early
revenue**.

This removes the requirement for early founder drawings/profit and
materially improves the project's ability to compound.

Revenue availability alone does **not** justify expenditure.

Meaningful expenditure should satisfy at least one of these tests:

1.  Does it measurably improve the learner product?
2.  Does it remove or materially reduce a real risk?
3.  Does it increase development/content throughput?
4.  Does it acquire customers at demonstrated favourable unit economics?

If none applies, retain the cash.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1.2 Pre-revenue spending target

The project should set an intentionally aggressive constraint:

> **Target: reach first paying users for less than £1,000 of incremental
> project cash expenditure, excluding AI subscriptions/tools the Product
> Owner already maintains for other purposes.**

This is a target, not a launch blocker.

Its purpose is to force disciplined architecture and purchasing
decisions.

The first sub-slice/private testing should be achievable using:

-   founder time;
-   existing AI subscriptions where available;
-   Git/repository tooling;
-   free or very low-cost hosting/database tiers;
-   low-volume transactional email;
-   existing lawfully held reference materials;
-   deterministic processing;
-   direct founder outreach.

No conventional startup team is required to test the hypothesis.

# 2. Important financial terminology

## ARPU --- Average Revenue Per User

The average revenue produced by one paying learner over a stated period.

Why it matters: 10,000 users paying £5 are a different business from
10,000 users paying £100.

## CAC --- Customer Acquisition Cost

How much it costs, on average, to obtain one new paying customer.

Examples include:

-   advertising;
-   affiliate/referral payments;
-   sales commission;
-   marketing spend.

Why it matters: a £50 first-year contribution cannot sustainably support
a £70 acquisition cost.

## LTV --- Lifetime Value

The contribution generated by a customer over their whole paid
relationship.

Why it matters: an electrician who subscribes repeatedly across 2365,
NVQ and 2391 may be worth much more than someone who subscribes for one
month before an exam.

## Gross margin

Revenue remaining after the direct/variable cost of delivering the
service.

It does **not** deduct all company expenses such as founder salary,
marketing staff or general administration.

## Contribution

For this model:

> net revenue after VAT minus payment processing and direct per-learner
> delivery/support cost.

This is the money available to pay for marketing, content, development,
salaries and profit.

## Churn

The rate at which subscribers cancel or stop paying.

Why it matters: high churn can destroy LTV even when the monthly
subscription price looks attractive.

## Unit economics

The economics of one customer/unit.

Why it matters: if one additional customer loses money, scaling makes
the problem larger rather than solving it.

# 2.1 Cash cost versus economic cost

These must never be conflated.

## Cash cost

Money actually leaving the company.

During bootstrap:

``` text
Founder development labour        £0 cash
Founder content labour            £0 cash
Founder outreach                  £0 cash
Founder product management        £0 cash
```

This is the correct basis for determining whether the project can be
bootstrapped.

## Economic cost

What those functions would cost if the company eventually had to pay
somebody to perform them.

Founder labour has substantial economic value even when its cash cost is
£0.

The mature model must therefore eventually price:

-   software engineering;
-   content production;
-   subject-matter review;
-   product management;
-   customer support;
-   marketing/sales;
-   security/compliance.

This ensures the business remains scalable beyond the founder.

# 2.2 Paid CAC versus founder-led acquisition

During initial validation:

> **Paid CAC target = £0.**

The initial acquisition strategy is:

-   direct social outreach;
-   relevant learner communities;
-   tutors/trainers;
-   electrical creators/influencers;
-   thought leaders;
-   referrals;
-   organic search/content;
-   free trial/access offered selectively to people capable of giving
    useful feedback or reach.

Founder time is not economically free, but it requires no cash payment.

Therefore early reporting should distinguish:

``` text
paid CAC
founder outreach hours
organic acquisition
referral acquisition
partner/influencer acquisition
```

Do not report "CAC = £0" later if substantial paid marketing or
commissions are introduced.

# 4. Recommended initial B2C pricing hypothesis

**B2C --- Business-to-Consumer** means individual learners paying
directly.

WP0.6 recommends testing one simple main paid tier rather than launching
with excessive pricing complexity.

## Free / diagnostic access

Possible free access should be sufficient to demonstrate the product's
intelligence but not replace the paid product.

Potentially:

-   initial diagnostic;
-   limited daily practice;
-   limited curriculum view;
-   a small number of targeted remediation sessions;
-   sample progress view.

This is a product-acquisition hypothesis, not yet an approved freemium
design.

## Paid learner tier

### Monthly

**£9.99/month including VAT**

### Annual

**£79.99/year including VAT**

Annual pricing is equivalent to approximately £6.67/month and gives a
meaningful incentive to commit.

### Why not £4.99?

The platform is intended to provide materially more value than a basic
revision app.

Underpricing would:

-   reduce perceived differentiation;
-   leave less room for content/support/security costs;
-   make paid acquisition harder;
-   make later international expansion/pricing changes difficult.

### Why not £19.99/month initially?

Free electrical resources are strong and willingness to pay for the
differentiated product remains unproven.

A higher tier could be introduced later for:

-   enhanced AI tutoring;
-   multiple professional verticals;
-   advanced readiness analytics;
-   premium specialist modules.

Do not build that complexity before demand is demonstrated.

# 5. VAT must be included in the business model

The UK VAT registration threshold remains **£90,000 taxable turnover**
for 2026/27.

The standard VAT rate is **20%** for most goods/services.

At meaningful commercial scale, consumer prices should therefore be
modelled as VAT-inclusive.

Example:

``` text
Learner pays:             £9.99
Revenue before VAT:       £8.325
VAT element:              £1.665
```

Therefore a £9.99 advertised price must **not** be modelled as £9.99
revenue.

Official sources:

-   HMRC VAT threshold: https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat
-   VAT rates: https://www.gov.uk/vat-rates

# 6. Payment costs

Stripe currently lists standard UK card Checkout pricing of:

> **1.5% + 20p per successful standard UK-card transaction**

Actual billing/subscription fees depend on the exact Stripe products
used and must be rechecked before implementation.

This fixed 20p component makes monthly billing more expensive than
annual billing.

Example:

``` text
Annual £79.99 payment:
percentage fee ≈ £1.20
fixed fee       £0.20
basic payment fee ≈ £1.40
```

A monthly subscriber incurs the fixed payment charge repeatedly.

For the Phase 0 model, a conservative **3.5% effective payment/billing
allowance** is used rather than pretending the basic card fee is the
only cost.

Source: https://stripe.com/gb/payments/checkout

# 7. Baseline paid-learner model

The base unit model assumes:

-   60% of paid learners use annual billing;
-   40% use monthly billing;
-   average monthly subscriber remains paid for 7 months in a year;
-   annual price = £79.99 including VAT;
-   monthly price = £9.99 including VAT.

This produces approximate average annual consumer billings of:

> **£75.97 per paid learner-year**

After 20% VAT:

> **£63.31 net company revenue per paid learner-year**

A **learner-year** here means the equivalent of one paying learner
consuming the platform for the modelled year. It allows monthly and
annual subscribers to be compared consistently.

This is an assumption to be replaced by real cohort data after
beta/early access.

# 8. Direct per-learner delivery-cost hypothesis

Base-case annual variable costs per paid learner are modelled as:

## Payment/billing

**£2.66**

Based on a conservative 3.5% effective allowance on consumer billings.

## AI inference

**£2.50**

This is a budget, not a vendor quote.

It assumes:

-   routine learning remains deterministic;
-   stored governed explanations are heavily reused;
-   live AI is optional/targeted;
-   AI usage is rate-limited and budgeted.

If AI costs £15--£30 per learner-year, the product architecture has
failed one of its core cost objectives.

## Database/compute/bandwidth allocation

**£1.50**

This is deliberately conservative at early-to-medium scale.

Current infrastructure pricing supports the plausibility of low per-user
costs:

-   Supabase Pro starts at \$25/month and currently includes up to
    100,000 monthly active users before MAU overage, although
    compute/storage scale separately;
-   Cloudflare Workers currently starts at \$5/month with very low
    request overage pricing.

These are examples, not architecture decisions.

## Authentication / transactional email allocation

**£0.50**

Passwordless email creates a real but small delivery cost if implemented
sensibly.

Postmark currently charges about \$15/month for 10,000 transactional
emails; other providers may be cheaper.

## Variable learner support allowance

**£3.00**

This represents an average support burden.

If the product requires large amounts of manual tutor/support
intervention, it stops behaving like the intended high-margin software
product.

# 10. Infrastructure scalability finding

The business does not require a huge infrastructure bill to reach
meaningful scale if the architecture remains deterministic-first.

Current vendor pricing demonstrates why.

## Supabase example

Current Pro pricing starts at \$25/month and includes:

-   100,000 monthly active users before MAU overage;
-   database/storage/bandwidth allowances;
-   daily backups.

This does **not** mean 100,000 active learners can run on a \$25 server.

Database compute, storage and workload will scale.

It does show that identity/user-count licensing is unlikely to impose a
large per-learner tax.

## Cloudflare example

Workers pricing currently begins at approximately \$5/month with 10
million requests included and low per-million request charges
thereafter.

Again, this is illustrative rather than an architecture decision.

### Conclusion

At 10,000 paying learners, even tens of thousands of pounds per year of
robust infrastructure would remain small relative to revenue.

**Content and customer acquisition are materially larger economic risks
than HTTP requests/database identities.**

# 19. Founder time must not be treated as free forever

AI-assisted development can make initial cash cost unusually low.

That is advantageous.

However, commercial modelling must distinguish:

-   **cash cost** --- money paid out;
-   **founder time/opportunity cost** --- time that has economic value
    even if no salary is initially drawn.

For Phase 0 and the first sub-slice it is reasonable to optimise cash
expenditure and use founder time heavily.

Before commercial scaling, the platform must demonstrate economics that
would remain viable if:

-   content specialists are paid;
-   developers are paid;
-   support staff are paid;
-   security/legal work is paid.

Otherwise the business has created a founder-dependent hobby rather than
a scalable company.

# 20. Three operating stages

The earlier conventional fixed-cost envelopes are replaced by a staged
bootstrap model.

## Stage A --- Founder bootstrap

Characteristics:

-   founder + AI development;
-   founder-led content development;
-   founder-led product management;
-   no employees;
-   no paid advertising;
-   direct/organic outreach;
-   very low user volumes initially;
-   free/low-cost infrastructure where appropriate;
-   professional expenditure deferred until justified by risk or
    revenue.

### Indicative incremental cash expenditure

The exact amount depends on which existing AI/tool subscriptions are
allocated to the project.

A deliberately lean first-year cash profile might include:

``` text
domain/DNS/basic email                  tens to low hundreds
hosting/database/CDN                    £0–low hundreds initially
transactional email                     £0–low hundreds
monitoring/backups/security tooling     £0–low hundreds
company/admin/accounting                only as required
payment processing                      transaction-based
marketing                               £0 paid initially
development labour                      £0 cash
initial content labour                  £0 cash
founder outreach                        £0 cash
```

### Target

> **\< £1,000 incremental cash to first paying user**, excluding
> already-held general AI subscriptions.

After first revenue, the project may exceed this figure where
reinvestment is justified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage B --- Revenue-funded professionalisation

Trigger:

Not a fixed user count.

Begin when:

-   revenue exists;
-   a specific expenditure has a strong product/risk/growth
    justification;
-   paying for it is better than the founder continuing to
    perform/accept the function personally.

Possible reinvestment:

-   better AI/development tooling;
-   selective specialist content review;
-   UX/design assistance;
-   legal/IP review;
-   independent security testing;
-   improved monitoring/infrastructure;
-   customer-support tooling;
-   content-production assistance;
-   carefully tested acquisition channels.

The strategic objective is:

> **Customers fund the transition from founder-built product to
> professionally assured product.**

The Product Owner is willing to reinvest up to 100% of revenue during
this stage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage C --- Scaled company

Only after product and demand are demonstrated should the company
deliberately purchase substantial permanent capability.

Potential expenditure:

-   engineering staff/contractors;
-   content specialists;
-   support;
-   security/compliance;
-   B2B sales;
-   marketing;
-   finance/operations;
-   internationalisation.

At this stage, annual fixed costs of £100k--£300k+ may become entirely
reasonable.

They are **not preconditions for launching the business**.

Potentially achievable with **tens of paying learners** while the

# 23. What 20,000 paying learners actually means

England has hundreds of thousands of learners in regulated vocational

# 25. Freemium versus trial

A **freemium** product has a permanently free tier with paid upgrades.

A **free trial** gives temporary access to paid functionality.

Both are plausible.

## Freemium advantage

Electrical has strong free competitors.

A useful free tier can:

-   build SEO/word-of-mouth;
-   demonstrate diagnosis;
-   reduce purchase anxiety;
-   create learner data;
-   act as acquisition funnel.

## Freemium risk

If free users can obtain enough value from unlimited basic questions,
they may never pay.

## Trial advantage

Clearer commercial conversion and simpler entitlements.

## Recommendation

Do not decide this from theory.

The first beta should measure:

-   diagnostic completion;
-   first-remediation "aha" moment;
-   return usage;
-   willingness to pay.

Then A/B test trial/freemium mechanics.

**A/B test** means showing two different product/pricing experiences to
comparable user groups and measuring which performs better.

# 26. Conversion assumptions

Phase 0 should **not** build the business case around a generic website
conversion percentage.

Traffic quality varies enormously.

Examples:

-   learner searching "2365 mock exam" has high intent;
-   casual YouTube traffic has lower intent;
-   college-assigned learner access behaves differently again.

Instead model channels individually after beta:

``` text
organic search
referrals
college/provider
creator partnership
paid search
social
direct
```

For every channel measure:

-   visitors/leads;
-   activated learners;
-   paid learners;
-   CAC;
-   retention;
-   LTV.

The enterprise is viable only if enough acquisition occurs through
low-cost/high-intent channels.

# 27. Security economics

World-class security has a cost and must be budgeted.

Likely expenditure eventually includes:

-   external penetration testing;
-   dependency/security tooling;
-   logging/monitoring;
-   backups;
-   incident-response preparation;
-   legal/privacy review;
-   cyber insurance;
-   security-focused contractor/engineering time.

A **penetration test** is an authorised security assessment where
specialists actively try to find exploitable weaknesses.

Why it matters:

> security cannot be claimed merely because automated tests pass.

The base operating-cost scenarios reserve room for this rather than
assuming security is free.

# 28. Passwordless-auth economics

Passwordless email remains commercially plausible.

Transactional-email costs are very small compared with learner revenue.

Postmark currently prices 10,000 monthly emails from around \$15/month.

Even if one active learner triggers several authentication/service
emails each month, this is unlikely to become a major unit cost.

The more important considerations are:

-   deliverability;
-   reliability;
-   abuse prevention/rate limiting;
-   account security;
-   user friction.

Do not choose the cheapest provider if sign-in emails regularly fail to
arrive.

# 29. Deterministic architecture creates economic leverage

Consider 10,000 learners answering 100 questions/month:

> **1,000,000 question interactions/month**

If every interaction requires an LLM call, cost and latency become
significant.

If most interactions are:

-   stored questions;
-   deterministic generation;
-   deterministic marking;
-   deterministic mastery updates;
-   stored explanations;

then the marginal cost of one more question becomes extremely small.

Live AI can be reserved for the minority of interactions that benefit
from it.

This is one of the strongest architectural-economic reasons to continue.

# 30. Content reuse creates the second economic leverage

The first vertical bears the cost of creating:

-   Maths foundations;
-   Physics foundations;
-   learner engine;
-   diagnostic engine;
-   question system;
-   provenance;

> **Marginal Vertical Cost Ratio**

Conceptually:

─────────────────────────

Revenue:

A relatively small number of successful provider relationships can
therefore equal thousands of individual monthly purchases.

For example:

> **£225,000 annual revenue**

## 25 providers × 500 learners

B2B is not "easy money".

# 33. Founder-led development and reinvestment strategy

Founder + AI development materially changes the capital requirement.

The intended progression is:

``` text
FOUNDER + AI
     ↓
minimal pre-revenue cash
     ↓
proving slice
     ↓
real learner testing
     ↓
first paying users
     ↓
cash-neutral operation
     ↓
up to 100% revenue reinvestment
     ↓
better product / content / assurance
     ↓
more revenue
     ↓
second vertical
     ↓
professionalisation
```

The company should not spend heavily merely to resemble a startup.

There is no requirement to:

-   hire a conventional development team before validation;
-   employ sales staff before proving demand;
-   buy enterprise infrastructure for tiny user volumes;
-   purchase expensive SaaS tools where simple alternatives suffice;
-   commission broad consulting work without a specific decision need;
-   extract founder income before the Product Owner chooses to do so.

## Reinvestment hierarchy

Early revenue should normally be considered in this order:

1.  keep the service reliable and secure;
2.  improve learner-facing product quality;
3.  remove high-value content/technical bottlenecks;
4.  obtain professional assurance when the risk justifies it;
5.  expand high-value knowledge/content;
6.  prove the second vertical;
7.  test scalable acquisition;
8.  only then add permanent organisational cost.

This is intended to preserve bootstrap discipline even after meaningful
cash begins accumulating.

# 33.1 Professional advice is a risk decision, not an automatic pre-revenue expense

The Product Owner may choose to make informed calls on legal/IP and
other professional-risk questions during early development rather than
incur professional fees immediately.

WP0.6 therefore does **not** include legal/IP advice or external
penetration testing as mandatory pre-revenue cash costs.

They remain governance/risk gates whose timing depends on:

-   what is being exposed publicly;
-   whether proprietary-derived content is being commercialised;
-   quantity/sensitivity of user data;
-   revenue/user scale;
-   Product Owner risk tolerance.

The platform should still be architected from day one so that later
review is straightforward.

Professional assurance should be purchased when its expected risk
reduction justifies the expenditure, ideally from earned revenue.

# 34. Kill / rethink conditions

The enterprise should be reconsidered if the first substantial learner
trials show several of the following.

## Product failure

Learners do not perceive root-cause remediation as materially more
useful than free mocks + general AI.

## Content failure

High-quality governed content requires so much manual specialist review
that vertical economics become unattractive.

## Acquisition failure

Blended sustainable CAC remains above roughly £40--£50 while learner
contribution/LTV stays near the current base case.

## Retention failure

Most users subscribe for one month only and no credible recurring
qualification relationship emerges.

## AI-cost failure

Routine learner use requires £15+ annual inference cost per learner to
feel competitive.

## Reuse failure

Engineering/second vertical requires rebuilding Maths/Physics/platform
knowledge rather than consuming shared assets.

## IP failure

Legal review determines that the scalable proprietary-reference
processing/content model requires licensing costs or restrictions
incompatible with the economics.

One negative result does not automatically kill the company, but several
would materially weaken the thesis.

# 35. Strong go signals

Conversely, the enterprise becomes substantially more attractive if
early testing shows:

-   learners clearly prefer diagnostic remediation to ordinary revision;
-   organic/referral acquisition produces CAC below £15--£20;
-   annual subscriptions are popular;
-   learners progress from one electrical module/qualification to
    another;
-   content review cost per approved assertion falls as tooling
    improves;
-   maths/physics assertions are heavily reused;
-   AI cost remains under £3--£5 per learner-year;
-   tutors/providers ask for cohort access;
-   question/report error rate is very low;
-   second-vertical marginal cost is materially below first-vertical
    cost.
