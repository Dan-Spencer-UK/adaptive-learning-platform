# Phase 0 --- WP0.1: UK Market Definition and Qualification-Family Longlist

**Status:** Draft for Product Owner review\
**Date:** 13 August 2026\
**Scope:** United Kingdom only\
**Purpose:** Define the market that Phase 0 will investigate before any
learner-volume, competitor, source-feasibility or commercial scoring
work determines the launch portfolio.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Decision this work package must make

WP0.1 answers:

> **What kinds of UK qualifications and learner markets are eligible to
> be considered for the platform, and which market families should
> proceed into quantitative Phase 0 research?**

It does **not** choose the launch verticals.

It establishes the research universe so that later work does not simply
confirm the subjects already familiar to the Product Owner.

The subsequent Phase 0 work packages will determine:

-   learner population;
-   commercial attractiveness;
-   competition;
-   knowledge/source feasibility;
-   intellectual-property risk;
-   content-production effort;
-   deterministic-learning suitability;
-   cross-qualification and cross-vertical knowledge reuse;
-   likely willingness to pay;
-   launch SAM and obtainable market.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Geographic scope

The initial commercial market is the **United Kingdom**.

Ireland is excluded from the initial Phase 0 launch-market model because
its population is too small to justify allowing it to influence the
first vertical selection. It may be added later as an adjacent
jurisdiction.

The UK is not one completely uniform qualifications market.

Relevant systems include:

-   England --- Ofqual-regulated qualifications, apprenticeships and
    Skills England occupational standards;
-   Wales --- Qualifications Wales regulates non-degree qualifications;
-   Scotland --- a distinct Scottish qualifications system, now overseen
    by Qualifications Scotland, which replaced SQA as the national
    awarding body on 1 February 2026;
-   Northern Ireland --- CCEA Regulation regulates relevant
    qualifications in Northern Ireland.

This matters because a qualification may be:

-   UK-wide;
-   offered in several UK nations;
-   England-specific;
-   Scotland-specific;
-   Wales-specific;
-   Northern Ireland-specific.

### Working launch-research approach

Phase 0 should use **England as the principal numerical market**,
because of its population and data availability, while recording where
the same qualification/product can also serve Wales, Scotland and
Northern Ireland.

Do not simply multiply an English learner figure by a UK population
factor.

Where material, the other nations should be quantified using their own
qualification systems/data.

## 3. Market definition

The proposed Phase 0 market is:

> **UK learners undertaking structured vocational, technical or
> professional qualifications where knowledge acquisition or revision is
> a meaningful component, the curriculum or required knowledge can be
> mapped, a substantial part of learning can be delivered or assessed
> digitally, and an adaptive learning product could materially improve
> preparation, knowledge retention or assessment performance.**

The platform is not initially intended to compete across all education.

### Initially in scope

-   vocational qualifications;
-   technical qualifications;
-   apprenticeships with substantial knowledge components;
-   professional/occupational qualifications;
-   trade qualifications;
-   qualification-linked compliance/regulatory knowledge;
-   Functional Skills Maths because of its role in vocational pathways
    and its value as reusable foundational knowledge.

### Initially outside the launch-market search

-   GCSEs as a general academic market;
-   A levels as a general academic market;
-   university degrees as a general market;
-   generic language learning;
-   primary/secondary general education;
-   recreational/non-qualification learning;
-   qualifications whose value is overwhelmingly dependent on practical
    observation and cannot be meaningfully supported by knowledge
    learning;
-   markets where lawful commercial content cannot realistically be
    created.

This exclusion is about launch focus, not a permanent product
limitation.

## 4. Product fit required for a candidate market

A large learner population alone is insufficient.

A candidate should ideally have most of the following characteristics.

### 4.1 Recurring learner demand

There should be a meaningful UK population entering or progressing
through the qualification/pathway every year.

### 4.2 Structured requirements

It should be possible to identify what learners are expected to know or
be able to demonstrate.

### 4.3 Meaningful knowledge component

Knowledge acquisition must matter sufficiently that a learning platform
can affect learner outcomes.

### 4.4 Digital assessability

A substantial part of the knowledge should be testable digitally through
mechanisms such as:

-   multiple choice;
-   numerical answers;
-   calculations;
-   ordering/sequencing;
-   matching;
-   interpretation;
-   scenario questions;
-   short structured answers;
-   diagram-based questions;
-   controlled AI-assisted marking where deterministic marking is
    unsuitable.

### 4.5 Adaptive-learning opportunity

The subject should contain enough structure and prerequisite
relationships for the platform to improve on a static question bank
through:

-   mastery tracking;
-   weakness identification;
-   misconception detection;
-   root-cause diagnosis;
-   targeted remediation;
-   spaced retrieval;
-   next-best-activity selection.

### 4.6 Repeat engagement

The learner should normally need sustained study over weeks or months
rather than a single short information lookup.

### 4.7 Economic motivation

Passing, progressing, qualifying or maintaining competence should have
sufficient value to create willingness to pay by:

-   learners;
-   employers;
-   colleges/training providers; or
-   another institutional buyer.

### 4.8 Expansion/reuse value

Preference should be given to knowledge investments that can support:

-   multiple qualifications;
-   multiple levels;
-   adjacent occupations;
-   shared foundational subjects;
-   future jurisdictions.

## 5. Electrical as the preferred proving vertical

Electrical is **not pre-selected as the commercial winner**, but it
receives a deliberate first-vertical execution advantage.

The Product Owner is currently undertaking City & Guilds 2365 Level 2/3
and 2357-related training and can therefore sense-check:

-   qualification structure;
-   terminology;
-   question realism;
-   explanations;
-   calculations;
-   learning pain points;
-   curriculum relevance;
-   whether weakness diagnosis is genuinely useful;
-   whether the learner experience feels credible.

This reduces early product-development risk.

### Domain Validation Advantage

Later scoring should therefore include a temporary factor called
**Domain Validation Advantage**.

This measures the value of having unusually strong Product Owner access
to a domain for the first proving vertical.

It should carry meaningful weight when choosing the **first vertical**,
but little or no weight when selecting mature-platform expansion
markets.

This prevents two opposite mistakes:

1.  selecting electrical merely because it is familiar; or
2.  ignoring the genuine economic value of being able to test the first
    product personally.

## 6. Qualification is not the same as market

Phase 0 must not assume that each qualification is an independent
commercial product.

For example:

``` text
                         ELECTRICAL DOMAIN
                                │
        ┌───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┐
        ↓                       ↓                       ↓
Entry/training             Apprenticeship        Post-qualification
qualifications             pathway               specialist awards
        │                       │                       │
      2365                    5357                2382 / 2391 / etc.
```

Much of the underlying knowledge can overlap.

Therefore later market analysis must measure both:

### Qualification-level opportunity

How many learners take a particular qualification?

and:

### Knowledge-domain opportunity

How many qualifications and learners can be served by substantially the
same governed knowledge investment?

This is central to the platform economics.

## 7. Intellectual-property and source-use gate

A candidate vertical does **not** require every authoritative reference
source to be freely licensed.

However, the platform must be capable of producing a lawful commercial
learning product without depending on unlicensed reproduction or
redistribution of proprietary material.

The working rule is:

> **Proprietary authoritative references may remain acknowledged sources
> of authority and provenance, while the platform independently authors
> its knowledge representations, questions, explanations, examples and
> teaching material.**

The project should distinguish:

``` text
AUTHORITATIVE REFERENCE
        ↓
verification / provenance
        ↓
INDEPENDENT KNOWLEDGE ASSERTION
        ↓
independently authored educational content
```

For example, a proprietary electrical standard or professional handbook
may be recorded as the authority for a requirement, including edition
and section/regulation reference, without assuming that its wording,
tables, diagrams or other protected expression can be copied into the
platform.

### Phase 0 must not ask only:

> "Are the sources free?"

It must ask:

> **"Can we lawfully build a complete and commercially credible learning
> corpus from this knowledge ecosystem without reproducing protected
> material for which we do not have rights?"**

### Automatic warning signs

A market requires deeper IP review where:

-   essential teaching depends on reproducing proprietary tables;
-   the assessment expects access to a proprietary publication;
-   diagrams/images are central and difficult to recreate independently;
-   the only credible source of required knowledge is a commercial work;
-   a generated question bank could effectively reconstruct/substitute
    for the proprietary source;
-   licensing terms restrict commercial use, automated processing or
    derived products;
-   qualification materials themselves have restrictive reuse terms.

A vertical should ultimately fail the source-feasibility gate if the
product cannot be made commercially credible without unacceptable IP
risk or commercially unattractive licensing.

Specialist UK IP advice should be obtained before launch for the
implemented source-use model.

## 8. Initial UK market-family longlist

The following families proceed to later Phase 0 screening unless removed
during Product Owner review.

### Tier A --- priority research candidates

These appear particularly compatible with the proposed product and
deserve detailed quantitative research.

#### A1. Electrical installation / electrotechnical

Representative ecosystem includes:

-   City & Guilds 2365 Electrotechnical Craft;
-   5357 electrotechnical/apprenticeship qualification;
-   2357 legacy/workplace route where still relevant to learners;
-   2382 Requirements for Electrical Installations;
-   2391 Inspection and Testing;
-   experienced-worker routes;
-   technical occupational entry;
-   design/verification and adjacent electrical qualifications.

**Why investigate:** high deterministic content; calculations; clear
prerequisite structure; regulatory knowledge; strong cross-qualification
reuse; Product Owner validation advantage.

**Known caution:** important proprietary standards/reference works mean
the provenance-versus-reproduction model must be tested carefully.

#### A2. Engineering

Representative areas:

-   mechanical engineering;
-   electrical/electronic engineering;
-   manufacturing;
-   maintenance;
-   engineering operations;
-   technical engineering qualifications;
-   apprenticeship knowledge requirements.

**Why investigate:** strong maths/science reuse; deterministic
calculations; broad qualification ecosystem; potentially large market.

#### A3. Plumbing and heating

Representative areas:

-   Level 2 plumbing;
-   Level 3 plumbing/heating;
-   plumbing and domestic heating apprenticeships;
-   building-services pathways;
-   specialist heating qualifications.

**Why investigate:** substantial trade market; good maths/science reuse;
strong adjacency to electrical; practical plus knowledge components.

#### A4. Accounting and bookkeeping

Representative ecosystem:

-   AAT Level 2 Certificate in Accounting;
-   AAT Level 3 Diploma in Accounting;
-   AAT Level 4 professional accounting pathway;
-   bookkeeping qualifications.

**Why investigate:** exceptionally structured knowledge; highly
deterministic assessment opportunities; meaningful willingness to pay;
long learner engagement; useful test of whether the platform generalises
beyond trades.

**Strategic value:** this is an important counterweight to a
construction-only roadmap.

#### A5. Automotive maintenance and repair

Representative areas:

-   light vehicle;
-   maintenance/repair;
-   diagnostics;
-   electrical/electronic systems;
-   EV/hybrid;
-   workplace competence pathways.

**Why investigate:** large occupational field; strong science/electrical
reuse; diagnostic reasoning may suit adaptive weakness modelling.

#### A6. Refrigeration / air-conditioning / heat pumps / HVAC

Representative areas:

-   refrigeration and air conditioning;
-   F-Gas-related knowledge;
-   heat pumps;
-   building-services specialisms.

**Why investigate:** strong overlap with electrical, thermodynamics,
pressure, energy and diagnostics; potentially high knowledge-reuse
ratio.

#### A7. Functional Skills Maths

**Why investigate:** very large learner population; almost entirely
deterministic core; highly reusable across trades/professions; directly
supports root-cause remediation.

**Important positioning hypothesis:** the platform may obtain more
differentiation from **vocational maths embedded in the learner's
trade** than by entering the generic Functional Skills revision market
head-on.

Functional Skills Maths should therefore be evaluated both as:

-   a standalone commercial market; and
-   a shared horizontal knowledge layer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Tier B --- investigate for hidden high-ROI opportunities

#### B1. Welding and fabrication

Good technical/mathematical fit; market scale and assessment structure
require quantification.

#### B2. Construction trades

Including carpentry/joinery, brickwork and related crafts.

Large vocational population, but the proportion of value addressable
through digital knowledge learning may vary significantly by trade.

#### B3. Health and social care

Potentially very large demand.

Cautions:

-   greater safety implications;
-   potentially more qualitative knowledge;
-   potentially heavier governance burden;
-   significant variation between qualifications and roles.

Should remain in the research universe but should not receive priority
merely because learner numbers are large.

#### B4. Early years / childcare

Large vocational/apprenticeship relevance but potentially less
deterministic than technical subjects.

#### B5. Hospitality / professional cookery

Potential scale and recurring learners; mixed practical/theoretical
suitability.

#### B6. Hairdressing / barbering / beauty

Established vocational qualification market; theory component may be
digitally addressable, but practical competence remains important.

#### B7. IT / digital / networking / cyber

Large and growing knowledge market.

Caution: intense competition from free resources, certification
providers, online courses and rapidly changing technical content may
materially weaken ROI.

#### B8. Business administration

Potentially large learner numbers, but adaptive deterministic learning
may offer less differentiation than in technical subjects.

#### B9. Logistics / warehousing / transport operations

Potentially large apprenticeship/occupational market. Knowledge depth
and willingness to pay require research.

#### B10. Management / leadership / project qualifications

Potentially higher professional willingness to pay but more qualitative
assessment and substantial established competition.

#### B11. Health and safety / compliance qualifications

Strong digital assessability and professional value.

Cautions include crowded markets, short course duration and
proprietary/reference requirements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Tier C --- deliberately excluded from initial launch research unless evidence changes

#### C1. GCSE general market

Huge market but extremely competitive and outside the initial vocational
focus.

#### C2. A-level general market

Same reasoning.

#### C3. University degree revision

Fragmented curricula and strong existing education ecosystem; poor
first-market focus.

#### C4. Generic language learning

Very large but exceptionally competitive and weakly related to the
proposed knowledge-platform advantage.

#### C5. Recreational learning

Weak qualification-linked willingness to pay and outside launch
strategy.

## 9. UK qualification-system implications for later research

WP0.2 must avoid treating the UK as a single dataset.

### England

Use Ofqual qualification data, Department for Education
apprenticeship/FE statistics, and Skills England occupational
standards/maps where relevant.

Skills England currently groups technical education into 15 occupational
routes and links apprenticeships, Higher Technical Qualifications and T
Levels to employer-recognised occupations.

### Wales

Qualifications Wales regulates non-degree qualifications in Wales and
maintains the Qualifications in Wales (QiW) database.

Where a qualification is also delivered in Wales, later analysis should
determine whether this adds a material learner population without
requiring a separate content build.

### Scotland

Scotland has a distinct qualifications system. Qualifications Scotland
replaced SQA as the national awarding body on 1 February 2026.

Later research must distinguish between:

-   qualifications shared with the rest of the UK;
-   Scottish Vocational Qualifications/Scottish pathways;
-   genuinely reusable underlying knowledge.

### Northern Ireland

CCEA Regulation oversees relevant regulated qualifications in Northern
Ireland. Available vocational statistics include
qualification/type/level/sector information and top qualifications.

Northern Ireland can be added where the same content investment is
reusable, but should not distort the primary England-led market ranking.

## 11. Research unit for WP0.2

WP0.2 should not merely ask:

> "How many engineering learners are there?"

For each priority family, research should progressively resolve:

``` text
MARKET FAMILY
    ↓
qualification / apprenticeship family
    ↓
specific qualification or standard
    ↓
annual starts / registrations / certificates
    ↓
estimated active learner population
    ↓
overlap with related qualifications
```

Where possible, collect:

-   annual starts;
-   active enrolments/participants;
-   annual certifications/achievements;
-   qualification duration;
-   awarding organisation;
-   nation(s) available;
-   level;
-   assessment mode;
-   current/closing/replacement status.

This will allow later SAM modelling to avoid double-counting learners
who take several qualifications in the same pathway.

## 13. Decisions established by WP0.1

Subject to Product Owner approval, WP0.1 establishes:

1.  **UK-only initial market.**
2.  **England-led quantitative research**, with Wales, Scotland and
    Northern Ireland added from their own systems where material.
3.  Initial focus on **vocational, technical and professional
    qualification learning**, not general academic education.
4.  Functional Skills Maths remains in scope because of both market size
    and cross-vertical reuse.
5.  Electrical receives a **Domain Validation Advantage** for
    first-vertical selection because the Product Owner can personally
    sense-check it.
6.  Electrical is **not automatically selected** if evidence later shows
    that it is commercially or legally unsuitable.
7.  Qualification-level demand and **domain-level reuse** must both be
    measured.
8.  Proprietary authoritative sources do **not** automatically
    disqualify a vertical.
9.  The hard IP requirement is the ability to create a lawful commercial
    product **without unlicensed reproduction/redistribution**.
10. Proprietary references may remain explicit provenance/authority
    while educational content is independently authored.
11. The longlist remains broad until quantitative evidence removes
    candidates.
12. No public launch vertical should be approved until its source/IP
    model passes a dedicated feasibility review.

## 15. Legal/source note

The UK Intellectual Property Office states that copyright protects
expression rather than the underlying idea, while copyright can protect
written works, illustrations, tables, databases and other original
material. UK fair-dealing/quotation exceptions are fact-specific and
consider matters including the amount used and whether the use
substitutes for the original market.

Accordingly, this project will use a conservative source policy and
obtain specialist review before commercial launch rather than assuming
that citation alone permits reproduction.
