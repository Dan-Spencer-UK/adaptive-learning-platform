**Status:** Draft for Product Owner review\

**Primary market:** England for numerical baseline; wider UK to be added
where directly evidenced\
**Depends on:** Approved WP0.1 market definition

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose

WP0.2 asks:

> **How large are the learner populations behind the candidate
> verticals, and what is the correct unit to use later when calculating
> the Serviceable Available Market (SAM)?**

**SAM --- Serviceable Available Market** means the portion of the total
market that the product could realistically serve given its subjects,
qualifications, geography and product scope.

This work package does **not** estimate revenue yet and does not select
the launch verticals. It establishes the population evidence that later
commercial scoring will use.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Important measurement problem

There is no single number called "the number of electrical learners".

Different official datasets measure different things:

-   **participants** --- unique learners taking part in a programme
    during a period;
-   **enrolments** --- learning aims/courses being studied; one learner
    can create several enrolments;
-   **starts** --- learners beginning an apprenticeship/programme;
-   **achievements** --- completed learning aims/programmes;
-   **certificates** --- qualifications awarded; one learner can earn
    several certificates.

These cannot be added together.

For example, a learner might study an electrical programme, take
Functional Skills Maths, complete several component qualifications and
later obtain another specialist electrical award. Counting all
certificates as separate customers would substantially overstate the
market.

### WP0.2 rule

Later SAM calculations should preferentially use:

1.  active learners/participants where available;
2.  starts × realistic programme duration where active learner counts
    are unavailable;
3.  qualification registrations where available;
4.  certificates/achievements as a demand proxy only.

Certificate numbers are useful evidence of market activity, but
**certificates are not customers**.

## 3. Top-level England market

The latest final Department for Education figures show 1,174,940 adults
participating in education and training in 2024/25, excluding
apprenticeships from that specific measure.

Of these, 740,620 participated in at least one regulated
education/training qualification.

There were 1,246,010 regulated-qualification enrolments.

This demonstrates the difference between learners and enrolments: the
same person can appear in more than one learning aim.

Ofqual separately recorded 5.259 million vocational/other qualification
certificates in England during the 2024/25 academic year.

The overall market is therefore easily large enough. The question is how
much of it belongs to high-fit qualification families.

# 4. Electrical --- first proving vertical

## 4.1 Correct market model: the electrical qualification ecosystem

Electrical must **not** be modelled primarily as a 54-month
apprenticeship market.

The commercial learner population includes four overlapping cohorts:

1.  **Apprenticeship learners** following a recognised electrotechnical
    apprenticeship route.
2.  **De novo adult/non-apprentice learners** entering the industry
    through technical study plus workplace NVQ/competence and AM2 rather
    than through an apprenticeship.
3.  **Experienced Worker / qualification-gap learners** who already have
    substantial industry experience but need recognised evidence of
    competence, additional underpinning knowledge and/or the applicable
    AM2-family assessment.
4.  **Post-qualification learners** taking wiring-regulations,
    inspection/testing, design/verification and specialist/upskilling
    learning.

**De novo** simply means a new entrant: someone qualifying from the
beginning rather than proving long-standing existing experience. It
matters because these learners are neither apprentices nor Experienced
Workers and would otherwise disappear from our market model.

A simplified ecosystem is:

``` text
                      BECOMING / REMAINING QUALIFIED

 APPRENTICESHIP       DE NOVO / ADULT       EXPERIENCED WORKER
      ROUTE          NON-APPRENTICE ROUTE         ROUTE
        │                    │                     │
 recognised L3       technical learning +    experience + required
 programme +          workplace NVQ /         knowledge/competence
 competence           competence route        evidence
        │                    │                     │
 applicable AM2       AM2 / applicable        AM2E / applicable
 assessment           assessment              assessment
        └────────────────────┼─────────────────────┘
                             ↓
                  RECOGNISED L3 COMPETENCE
                             ↓
                  ECS GOLD CARD ELIGIBILITY
                             ↓
                  POST-QUALIFICATION LEARNING
                             │
              BS 7671 / inspection & testing /
              design / EV / solar / storage / CPD
```

## 4.2 Apprenticeship route

The current England Installation and Maintenance Electrician
apprenticeship (ST0152) is Level 3, approved for new starts and has a
typical duration of 54 months plus an assessment period.

That long lifecycle is commercially attractive, but apprenticeship
participation is only one component of the addressable electrical
market.

## 4.3 De novo adult/non-apprentice NVQ route

A separate population consists of learners entering electrical work
without following a funded apprenticeship.

A learner may undertake technical qualifications, gain
employment/workplace evidence, complete a recognised Level 3
NVQ/competence route and undertake AM2 or the applicable competence
assessment.

This is a strategically important cohort and must be quantified
separately in the final SAM work.

The market model must therefore not use "non-apprentice" as shorthand
for "Experienced Worker".

## 4.4 Experienced Worker and qualification-gap routes

Experienced workers form another distinct cohort.

Some have extensive practical experience but lack the currently
recognised qualification/evidence package required for formal industry
recognition or ECS Gold Card eligibility.

Depending on their history and route, they may require an Experienced
Worker Assessment, additional underpinning knowledge, recognised
competence evidence and an AM2-family assessment such as AM2E.

There is also a broader **qualification-gap** population: people with
historic qualifications or substantial training whose existing evidence
does not map neatly to current recognition requirements.

This group may have unusually strong motivation to study because formal
recognition can affect employment opportunities and site access.

## 4.5 Gold Card interpretation

The project must **not** state that every already-qualified electrician
now has to take AM2 to obtain an ECS Gold Card.

Requirements depend on the person's qualification history and evidence.

For market purposes, the important point is that recognised Level 3
competence is required and that some new entrants, Experienced Workers
and legacy/qualification-gap electricians require an applicable
AM2-family assessment or additional recognised evidence.

## 4.6 Post-qualification electrical market

Already-qualified electricians remain potential learners.

Relevant learning can include:

-   current BS 7671 requirements;
-   inspection and testing;
-   initial verification;
-   periodic inspection;
-   design and verification;
-   EV charging;
-   solar PV;
-   battery storage;
-   heat pumps/building services;
-   future regulation updates and specialist CPD.

Electrical should therefore be evaluated as a potential **career-long
knowledge domain**, not merely an initial-qualification revision
product.

## 4.7 Required electrical SAM cohorts

The final numerical model should attempt to quantify separately:

### Cohort A --- Apprenticeship

Annual starts, active learners, duration and assessment stages.

### Cohort B --- De novo/non-apprentice

Technical-course learners, NVQ/competence-route learners and AM2
candidates, with overlap identified.

### Cohort C --- Experienced Worker / qualification-gap

Experienced Worker Assessment registrations, applicable AM2-family
candidates and other recognition-gap learners.

### Cohort D --- Post-qualification

Wiring-regulations, inspection/testing, design/verification and
specialist/upskilling learners.

These populations **must not simply be added together** because the same
person may appear in several cohorts over time.

## 4.8 Broad ecosystem evidence

Construction, Planning and the Built Environment regulated adult
enrolments increased from 39,950 in 2019/20 to **62,570 in 2024/25**.

That is not an electrical learner count, but it confirms a substantial
and growing surrounding training ecosystem.

Exact electrical population remains a qualification/pathway-level
extraction task.

## 4.9 Electrical conclusion

**Market-size status: PROMISING AND STRATEGICALLY ATTRACTIVE; exact
unique learner population remains unresolved.**

Electrical remains the preferred first full vertical because of:

-   several routes into recognised qualification;
-   a long potential learner lifecycle;
-   a continuing post-qualification market;
-   strong deterministic learning content;
-   cross-qualification reuse;
-   substantial maths/science reuse;
-   Product Owner Domain Validation Advantage.

# 5. Engineering and manufacturing

Adult regulated FE enrolments in **Engineering and Manufacturing
Technologies** grew by 4,480 in 2024/25, a **9.4% annual increase**.

That implies approximately **52,100 regulated adult enrolments** in
2024/25, compared with about 47,700 the previous year.

Separately, Ofqual recorded **38,120 certificates** in
technical/applied-general engineering and manufacturing qualifications
in 2024/25, up **9.1%** from 34,925.

These measures cover different populations and must not be added.

### Interpretation

Engineering has:

-   a meaningful existing learner population;
-   current growth;
-   numerous sub-disciplines;
-   substantial shared maths/science;
-   strong potential overlap with electrical.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: STRONG.**

Engineering remains a front-running expansion vertical.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Plumbing, heating and wider construction

Adult regulated FE enrolments in **Construction, Planning and the Built
Environment** reached **62,570 in 2024/25**, up from 39,950 in 2019/20.

The latest year increased by 3,190 enrolments, or **5.4%**.

Ofqual separately recorded **18,175 technical/applied-general
certificates** in the broad construction subject area during 2024/25.

Again, these are different populations.

Plumbing/heating is only one subset of construction, so 62,570 cannot be
treated as plumbing learners.

### Interpretation

The wider construction training market:

-   is substantial;
-   has shown sustained growth;
-   contains several potential platform verticals;
-   shares knowledge with electrical/HVAC.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: STRONG ECOSYSTEM; plumbing-specific population
still requires qualification-level extraction.**

Plumbing/heating proceeds.

# 7. Functional Skills Maths

This is the clearest quantified market in the current official data.

Ofqual recorded **170,530 Functional Skills Mathematics certificates**
in England in 2024/25.

Across all Functional Skills subjects there were 326,060 certificates.

By level across all subjects:

-   Entry Level: 143,155;
-   Level 1: 77,210;
-   Level 2: 105,690.

The maths certificate count fell from 175,830 in 2023/24 to 170,530 in
2024/25.

There is also a major policy change.

From February 2025, apprentices aged 19+ without Level 2 English/maths
are no longer universally required to achieve those qualifications to
complete their apprenticeship. DfE reports apprenticeship-associated
Level 2 maths participation falling from 81,150 to 35,840 at the
equivalent in-year point.

### Interpretation

Functional Skills Maths is unquestionably a large market, but the
apprenticeship-linked component is undergoing structural contraction.

That strengthens the case for treating maths in **two separate ways**:

1.  standalone Functional Skills revision market; and
2.  reusable foundational maths inside electrical, engineering,
    plumbing, automotive and other verticals.

The second use does not depend on the learner formally taking Functional
Skills.

A learner can have an algebra weakness even if they are not enrolled in
a maths qualification.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: VERY LARGE, but standalone apprenticeship-linked
demand has policy risk.**

As a shared horizontal knowledge layer, maths remains exceptionally
attractive.

# 8. Accounting / AAT

The broad adult FE subject area **Business, Administration and Law**
represented 8.1% of regulated enrolments in 2024/25.

Applying 8.1% to the 1,246,010 regulated enrolments gives an indicative
broad subject-area volume of approximately **101,000 enrolments**.

This is not an accounting figure.

Ofqual's technical/applied-general subset recorded 96,745 certificates
in Business, Administration and Law, but this includes many
non-accounting qualifications.

AAT also sits partly outside the narrow technical/applied-general
performance-table subset, so the zero AAT figure in Ofqual's specific
technical/applied-general table must **not** be interpreted as zero AAT
learners.

Current apprenticeship vacancies continue to show the Level 3 Assistant
Accountant pathway being delivered with AAT qualifications.

### Interpretation

The broad finance/business learner ecosystem is large enough to justify
deeper analysis, but WP0.2 does not yet have a defensible unique AAT
learner count from the official sources examined.

This is a data gap, not evidence of a small market.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: PROBABLY STRONG; exact AAT population
unresolved.**

AAT remains a priority candidate because its product fit is unusually
good.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Automotive

The available broad official tables combine automotive with larger
engineering/manufacturing classifications and therefore do not yet
provide a defensible unique automotive learner population.

However, the Institute of the Motor Industry remains an active
Ofqual-regulated awarding organisation, and automotive
apprenticeship/qualification routes are clearly active.

### Interpretation

Automotive cannot yet be ranked numerically against electrical or
plumbing from broad sector data without risking false precision.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: UNRESOLVED BUT PLAUSIBLY MATERIAL.**

Keep in the shortlist and obtain qualification/apprenticeship-level
figures before WP0.5 scoring.

# 10. HVAC / refrigeration / heat pumps

HVAC/RAC and heat-pump learning is distributed across building-services,
engineering, refrigeration and specialist qualifications rather than
appearing as one clean statistical category.

Broad construction and engineering populations are both growing, but
those totals cannot be allocated to HVAC.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: SMALLER/UNCERTAIN AS A STANDALONE VERTICAL,
potentially very attractive as an adjacency.**

Its strongest economic feature may be reuse of electrical, maths,
thermodynamics and building-services knowledge rather than raw
standalone learner volume.

# 11. Welding and fabrication

Welding/fabrication is similarly embedded within the broad Engineering
and Manufacturing Technologies category.

The parent category has approximately 52,100 adult regulated enrolments
and grew 9.4% in 2024/25.

That demonstrates a healthy ecosystem but does not provide a
welding-specific population.

### WP0.2 conclusion

**Market-size status: UNRESOLVED; retain as challenger.**

# 12. Other longlist markets

The official adult FE data confirms that some lower-priority families
are numerically very large.

Health, Public Services and Care represented **19.6%** of regulated
enrolments in 2024/25 despite falling 8.9% year on year.

This corresponds to roughly **244,000 broad subject-area enrolments**.

Business, Administration and Law represented approximately **101,000**
broad enrolments.

These figures demonstrate why Phase 0 cannot select markets using
population alone.

Health/social care may have several times the learner population of a
technical niche while still being less attractive because of:

-   content complexity;
-   safety/governance burden;
-   qualitative assessment;
-   weaker deterministic advantage;
-   potentially lower knowledge reuse.

Large market ≠ best first market.

# 14. What this tells us already

The research changes the picture in several useful ways.

## 14.1 There is no shortage of potential UK learners

England alone has:

-   740,620 adult participants in regulated education/training
    qualifications;
-   1.246 million regulated adult enrolments;
-   5.259 million vocational/other certificates annually.

The commercial challenge is choosing the right slice, not finding a
market that exists.

## 14.2 Technical markets are growing

Both Engineering/Manufacturing and Construction/Built Environment grew
materially in the latest final adult FE data.

This is favourable for the electrical-first strategy.

## 14.3 Maths is strategically important but has changed

170,530 annual maths Functional Skills certificates is a substantial
market.

However, the 2025 apprenticeship policy change means we should **not**
build a market thesis based on every adult apprentice being forced
through Level 2 maths.

The reusable maths/remediation layer remains valuable regardless.

## 14.4 Long qualification duration matters

Electrical's current 54-month apprenticeship illustrates why annual
starts or certificates can badly understate the number of learners who
might subscribe during a given year.

Later SAM modelling needs an **active learner stock**, not merely annual
completions.

## 14.5 We need qualification-level extraction before revenue modelling

Broad subject-area statistics are useful for screening but inadequate
for calculating SAM.

We must not say:

> "There are 62,570 construction enrolments, therefore there are 62,570
> electrical/plumbing customers."

That would be false precision.

# 15. Required detailed dataset for later SAM model

For each shortlisted qualification/pathway, the numerical model should
eventually contain:

-   qualification/standard identifier;
-   qualification name;
-   awarding organisation;
-   nation;
-   current status;
-   level;
-   annual starts/registrations;
-   active learners where available;
-   annual achievements/certificates;
-   typical duration;
-   overlap with other qualifications;
-   estimated unique learners;
-   likely learner lifecycle;
-   whether learner pays personally, employer pays, or provider pays.

For electrical specifically, build a pathway model rather than a flat
qualification list.

Example:

``` text
College / pre-employment
    2365 / equivalent
          ↓
Apprenticeship / occupational competence
    ST0152 + relevant qualification
          ↓
Qualified electrician
    2382 / 2391 / specialist CPD
```

The same individual may appear at several stages over time.

# 17. Data limitations

WP0.2 has deliberately not manufactured exact numbers where official
broad tables do not support them.

Important limitations:

1.  England has the strongest accessible current data; wider-UK figures
    remain to be layered in.
2.  Ofqual certificate counts exclude apprenticeship assessment
    qualifications from the vocational/other release.
3.  FE enrolments are not unique learners.
4.  Subject-area totals include qualifications irrelevant to our
    product.
5.  Apprentices can study additional qualifications and therefore appear
    in several datasets.
6.  Some privately funded/professional learners may not appear in
    DfE-funded FE participation statistics.
7.  Qualification reform means old and replacement qualifications can
    coexist temporarily.
8.  Functional Skills apprenticeship demand has been materially affected
    by the February 2025 policy change.

These limitations must remain visible in later modelling.

# 18. WP0.2 conclusions

The evidence is sufficient to establish:

1.  The England vocational market is comfortably large enough for the
    proposed business.
2.  Technical/construction markets are not marginal niches; relevant
    broad FE enrolments are in the tens of thousands annually.
3.  Construction/built-environment adult regulated enrolments have risen
    from 39,950 to 62,570 in five years.
4.  Engineering/manufacturing adult regulated enrolments grew about 9.4%
    in the latest final year to roughly 52,100.
5.  Functional Skills Maths produced 170,530 certificates in 2024/25.
6.  Functional Skills Maths has policy risk as a standalone
    apprenticeship-driven market because adult apprenticeship maths
    requirements changed in 2025.
7.  Electrical's 54-month apprenticeship duration means active learner
    stock is likely much larger than a one-year starts/completions
    figure would suggest.
8.  Electrical, engineering, plumbing/building services, accounting and
    maths all remain viable enough to continue Phase 0.
9.  Automotive, HVAC and welding remain credible challengers but require
    more granular numbers.
10. Broad population should **not** determine vertical selection by
    itself.
11. Qualification-level and apprenticeship-standard extraction is
    required before the final SAM/SOM model.

# 19. Recommendation and agreed first-development sequence

Electrical remains the first full subject vertical.

However, the agreed architecture/development sequence is now:

``` text
FOUNDATIONAL MATHS KNOWLEDGE CORE
          │
          ▼
SMALL END-TO-END DEVELOPMENT SUB-SLICE
          │
          ▼
ELECTRICAL --- FIRST FULL VERTICAL
          │
          ├── apprenticeship
          ├── de novo/non-apprentice NVQ
          ├── Experienced Worker / qualification-gap
          └── post-qualification learning
          │
          ▼
ENGINEERING / PLUMBING / HVAC / OTHER VERTICALS
```

## Foundational Maths Knowledge Core

Maths should exist as an independent reusable knowledge domain, not as
"electrical maths".

It should ultimately be capable of supporting both:

-   standalone foundational maths qualifications, including Functional
    Skills Maths where commercially justified; and
-   prerequisite/remediation knowledge for Electrical, Engineering,
    Plumbing/HVAC and future verticals.

For example, rearranging an equation should be represented once as maths
knowledge and mapped to every curriculum that requires that capability.

This gives the platform a shared foundation for cross-domain weakness
diagnosis.

## First development sub-slice

The first implemented **vertical slice** will deliberately be smaller
than the eventual Electrical/Maths product.

A vertical slice means a small piece that works through the whole system
from source knowledge to the learner-facing application.

The initial sub-slice should prove:

``` text
governed maths + electrical knowledge
        ↓
curriculum mapping
        ↓
questions
        ↓
learner answer
        ↓
marking and mastery
        ↓
weakness diagnosis
        ↓
targeted maths/electrical remediation
        ↓
retest
        ↓
progress / next activity
        ↓
secure learner-facing SaaS
```

The purpose is to validate the complete four-workstream architecture
before investing in complete subject coverage.

After that sub-slice works well, development expands horizontally into
the broader Foundational Maths Knowledge Core and complete first
Electrical vertical.

This is now the agreed first-development strategy.
