# Phase 0 --- WP0.3: UK Competitor and Existing-Product Analysis

**Status:** Draft for Product Owner review\
**Date:** 13 August 2026\
**Depends on:** Approved WP0.1 and WP0.2\
**Agreed first product direction:** Foundational Maths Knowledge Core →
small Electrical/Maths end-to-end development sub-slice → Electrical as
first full vertical

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Purpose

WP0.3 asks:

> **What do learners already have, how good is it, where are the genuine
> product gaps, and what must our product do better before launch?**

This is not simply a list of companies.

The important question is whether the proposed product has a defensible
learner-facing advantage over:

-   free websites;
-   question banks;
-   mobile revision apps;
-   textbooks;
-   YouTube;
-   training-provider portals;
-   awarding-body resources;
-   AI chatbots;
-   adaptive learning platforms.

A market with weak competitors is attractive, but a strong competitor
can also validate demand.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Executive outcome

The competitive picture is more nuanced than the original hypothesis.

## Electrical

There is **substantial revision content**, but it is fragmented.

The strongest electrical products found are still primarily:

-   mock-exam engines;
-   topic quizzes;
-   static revision resources;
-   textbooks;
-   training-provider courses;
-   reference-navigation practice.

However, one free entrant --- **Sparky Tests** --- is considerably
better than a basic question bank and should be treated as a serious
product benchmark.

The electrical market does **not** appear to have a clear, dominant
learner platform that combines:

-   qualification/pathway mapping;
-   persistent mastery modelling;
-   prerequisite/root-cause diagnosis;
-   cross-domain maths weakness detection;
-   targeted remediation;
-   spaced retrieval;
-   adaptive next-best activity;
-   career-long electrical learning;
-   transparent authoritative provenance.

That remains our principal opportunity.

## Foundational / Functional Skills Maths

Competition is materially stronger.

This market already contains:

-   polished consumer revision platforms;
-   progress tracking;
-   topic weakness identification;
-   AI tutors;
-   institutional adaptive learning;
-   diagnostic assessment;
-   personalised learning paths.

Most importantly, **CENTURY already provides genuine adaptive Functional
Skills learning and gap identification**.

Therefore:

> **Our differentiation cannot be "adaptive maths revision".**

That already exists.

Our distinctive maths proposition is instead:

> **A reusable foundational maths knowledge layer that can diagnose and
> remediate mathematical weaknesses inside vocational learning, while
> also being capable of supporting standalone maths qualifications.**

Example:

``` text
Electrical learner fails voltage-drop calculation
        ↓
platform distinguishes:
electrical concept understood
but formula transposition weak
        ↓
targeted foundational algebra remediation
        ↓
simple maths retest
        ↓
electrical-context retest
        ↓
both maths and electrical mastery updated
```

That cross-domain diagnostic loop is much more strategically interesting
than building another generic Functional Skills website.

## Overall Phase 0 conclusion

The competitor evidence **supports Electrical + Maths as the first
product direction**, but changes the positioning:

-   Electrical is the first commercial/domain vertical.
-   Foundational Maths is shared platform infrastructure and a future
    standalone product.
-   We should not lead with "question bank".
-   We should not lead with "AI tutor".
-   We should not lead with "personalised/adaptive maths".
-   We should lead with **evidence-backed knowledge, deep weakness
    diagnosis and targeted learning across qualification/domain
    boundaries**.

# 3. Competitor categories

The market contains several different competitor types.

## 3.1 Direct revision competitors

Products specifically helping learners pass the same qualifications.

Examples:

-   electrical mock-exam sites;
-   2365 apps;
-   18th Edition simulators;
-   Functional Skills revision platforms.

## 3.2 Training providers

Providers sell complete courses and often bundle:

-   video;
-   tutor support;
-   revision;
-   mock exams;
-   assessment preparation.

They compete for learner spend even if their software is not
sophisticated.

## 3.3 Awarding bodies / assessment organisations

City & Guilds, AAT and NET provide official materials or learner
support.

These have enormous authority but are not necessarily designed as
best-in-class adaptive revision products.

## 3.4 Books and reference works

Textbooks and exam-preparation books remain serious competitors because
they are:

-   trusted;
-   relatively cheap;
-   familiar;
-   often qualification-specific.

## 3.5 Free content

YouTube, forums and free websites have effectively zero price friction.

A paid product must be materially better than assembling these resources
manually.

## 3.6 General AI

Learners are already using ChatGPT/Gemini-style tools to:

-   explain difficult topics;
-   generate practice questions;
-   diagnose errors;
-   ask follow-up questions.

Community discussions specifically show electrical learners using
general AI to generate additional mock questions and focus on difficult
areas.

Therefore "we have AI" is not differentiation.

Our advantage must come from the governed knowledge/product system
around AI.

# 4. Electrical competitor analysis

## 4.1 Sparky Tests

**Position:** strongest free direct benchmark found.

Current public offering includes:

-   1,100-question BS 7671 mock/practice bank;
-   515-question Guidance Note 3 inspection/testing bank;
-   short quizzes;
-   topic practice;
-   full mock exams;
-   instant feedback;
-   explanations;
-   references back to relevant books/standards;
-   current-source alignment;
-   no required sign-up for basic use;
-   free access.

Community recommendations in 2026 specifically praise:

-   large question pools;
-   realistic questions;
-   exact book/page/table/paragraph guidance;
-   calculation explanations;
-   ability to practise by section;
-   usefulness for learning how to navigate BS 7671/GN3 rather than
    memorising answers.

### Strengths

-   free;
-   extremely low friction;
-   large current question banks;
-   source/reference navigation;
-   immediate explanations;
-   qualification relevance;
-   useful for open-book exams;
-   clearly understands electrician exam behaviour.

### Weaknesses / apparent gaps

From the public product examined, it is primarily a **practice-test
system**, not a complete adaptive learner model.

No clear evidence was found of:

-   persistent concept-level mastery across qualifications;
-   prerequisite graphs;
-   root-cause weakness inference;
-   cross-domain maths remediation;
-   spaced-retrieval scheduling;
-   learner-specific next-best learning sequence;
-   career-path/qualification planning;
-   integrated learning modules driven by diagnosed weakness.

### Competitive implication

We **must beat Sparky Tests on learning intelligence**, not merely on
number of questions.

A paid product that simply offers 1,500 questions instead of its 1,100
free questions would have a weak proposition.

## 4.6 LearnZone Media / complete course competition

LearnZone's current 18th Edition online offering demonstrates the

Public pricing found:

-   online course/tutor package around £98 + VAT;
-   course plus exam around £219 + VAT;
-   premium/tutor/exam packages higher again.

Features include:

-   structured course;
-   revision pack;
-   exam simulator;
-   mock test;
-   tutor support;
-   one-year access.

### Implication

There is a broad willingness-to-pay range:

``` text
FREE
  ↓
£5–£20 specialist revision tools
  ↓
£100+ supported online learning
  ↓
£200–£350+ course/exam packages
```

Our eventual pricing should be positioned according to **outcome
value**, not question count.

-   aligned across several electrical pathways including 5357, 8202 and

# 6. AM2 / NVQ / route-to-qualified-status competition

## 6.1 NET official preparation

NET publishes:

-   pre-assessment manuals;
-   compulsory readiness/self-assessment checklists;
-   descriptions of required competence;
-   preparation guidance.

The checklist explicitly asks learners to assess both **knowledge and
practical experience** before assessment.

### Product implication

We should not pretend software can replace practical competence.

For AM2-type preparation, the platform can help with:

-   underpinning knowledge;
-   sequence/procedure recall;
-   test interpretation;
-   fault diagnosis;
-   regulation/reference familiarity;
-   readiness self-assessment;
-   identifying areas requiring real-world practice.

But practical installation/testing competence remains outside what a
purely digital product can certify.

This honesty should strengthen learner trust.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.2 Training providers / NVQ providers

Providers such as XS Training compete through:

-   remote portfolio support;
-   assessor interaction;
-   observations;
-   professional discussions;
-   complete NVQ progression;
-   additional online learning.

They are primarily **qualification delivery providers**, not direct
substitutes for our knowledge engine.

Potentially, they become future **B2B customers or partners** rather
than pure competitors.

**B2B** means *business-to-business*: selling the platform to a college,
training provider or employer rather than directly to an individual
learner.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.3 AM2 preparation gap

Community discussions repeatedly show learners seeking:

-   AM2 study guides;
-   refresher courses;
-   mock preparation;
-   concise revision material;
-   guidance on common failure points.

One discussion described a training centre charging £200 for a short
period of online AM2 preparation.

A March 2026 learner post said existing AM2 revision material felt
scattered and difficult to follow, motivating them to create their own
revision pack.

### Implication

AM2 preparation may be an attractive later electrical module, especially
when linked to the learner's existing mastery record.

However, it should not become the first development sub-slice because it
combines practical and theoretical readiness.

# 7. Electrical learner pain points visible in community evidence

Recent UK electrician discussions repeatedly reveal:

### Fragmentation

Learners ask where to find useful revision resources rather than naming
one obvious dominant platform.

### Difficult maths/science

2365 electrical science and formulas are repeatedly identified as
difficult.

### Mock-test dependence

Learners commonly prepare by repeatedly searching for mock exams.

### General AI workaround

Learners already ask general AI to generate similar questions and
explain weak areas.

### Reference navigation

For 18th Edition and 2391, success involves knowing **where to find

### NVQ workload

Learners describe the NVQ portfolio process as burdensome and
potentially overwhelming.

### Implication

Our product opportunity is not merely "more content".

It is to organise the learner's knowledge journey.

## 8.3 CENTURY

CENTURY is the most important strategic competitor found for our maths
layer.

For FE colleges it advertises:

-   Functional Skills Maths Entry 1--Level 2;
-   adaptive initial assessment;
-   automatic gap identification;
-   personalised Recommended Paths;
-   targeted content;
-   automatic marking;
-   teacher dashboards;
-   learner progress;
-   personalised assignments;
-   integrations with OneFile, Aptem, Tribal and other systems.

CENTURY says it works with **100+ UK colleges**.

Its Smart Initial Assessment is adaptive and aims to establish
Functional Skills level in roughly 30 minutes.

It explicitly markets the ability to:

-   identify knowledge gaps;
-   identify misconceptions;
-   tailor material to individual needs;
-   continually adapt the learner pathway.

### This changes our competitive thesis

We cannot claim:

> "Competitors are static question banks while we identify weaknesses."

That would be false in maths.

CENTURY already does meaningful adaptive learning.

### Our potential differentiation from CENTURY

Our proposed architecture goes in a different direction:

**CENTURY model, broadly:**

``` text
Maths curriculum
    ↓
diagnose maths learner
    ↓
personalised maths path
```

**Our proposed model:**

``` text
SHARED KNOWLEDGE GRAPH / ASSERTION SYSTEM

          Maths
            ▲
            │ prerequisite relationships
            │
Electrical ─┼─ Engineering ─ Plumbing ─ HVAC
            │
            ▼
      learner mastery state
```

The intended advantage is **cross-domain causal diagnosis**.

Example:

> A learner is not simply "weak in electrical science".\
> The platform identifies that the repeated electrical failure
> originates in ratio/proportion or algebra, remediates that
> foundational capability, and then verifies transfer back into the
> electrical context.

That is the product hypothesis we need to prove.

-   Green Light tests;

-   study plans;

-   progress/activity;
-   videos;
-   podcasts.

work using red/amber/green status.

### Implication

Accounting remains commercially interesting, but the competitive bar is
high because the awarding body itself provides:

-   official practice;

substantially deeper adaptive/remediation value than official AAT
support.

# 11. Competitive moat hypothesis

A **moat** is something that makes a product difficult for competitors
to copy or replace.

The moat should **not** be:

-   number of questions;
-   having an AI chatbot;
-   having mock exams;
-   having progress bars;
-   having flashcards;
-   having videos.

All are readily reproducible.

The potential moat is the combination of:

## 11.1 Governed knowledge asset

A large, maintained set of:

-   assertions;
-   provenance;
-   curriculum mappings;
-   prerequisites;
-   misconceptions;
-   question mappings;
-   version relationships.

## 11.2 Learner evidence graph

Over time the platform accumulates evidence about:

-   what the learner knows;
-   confidence of that conclusion;
-   where errors occur;
-   which misconceptions recur;
-   prerequisite weaknesses;
-   retention/decay;
-   transfer across contexts.

## 11.3 Cross-domain reuse

The same foundational knowledge can support multiple verticals.

Competitors building qualification-by-qualification question banks
repeatedly rebuild overlapping content.

## 11.4 Learning decision engine

The platform decides what should happen next based on evidence rather
than presenting a catalogue.

## 11.5 Provenance and trust

The system can explain:

-   why something is taught;
-   which curriculum requires it;
-   which authority supports it;
-   which version applies.

This becomes particularly valuable in regulated technical domains.

# 12. What we must NOT claim as unique

Phase 0 evidence means the following claims would be unsafe:

> "No one identifies learner weaknesses."

False --- CENTURY and others do.

> "No one provides personalised learning."

False.

> "No one uses AI tutors."

False --- even inexpensive revision apps do.

> "Electrical learners have no good free mock exams."

False --- Sparky Tests is a significant free resource.

> "Question banks are poor."

Too broad. Some are large, current and well explained.

Our positioning must be more precise.

# 13. Genuine differentiation hypothesis

The strongest current proposition is:

> **A qualification-aware learning system that understands the
> underlying knowledge structure, continuously models what the learner
> actually knows, diagnoses the root cause of errors across subject
> boundaries, and automatically teaches and retests the knowledge needed
> next.**

For Electrical/Maths:

> **Don't just tell me I got an electrical question wrong. Work out why
> I got it wrong, teach the missing electrical or mathematical
> prerequisite, and prove I can now apply it.**

This is materially different from:

> "Here are more questions about the topic you scored badly in."

That distinction should drive product design.

# 14. Competitive requirements for the first development sub-slice

The first small Electrical/Maths sub-slice must prove the distinctive
loop, not merely demonstrate CRUD screens and quizzes.

**CRUD** means *Create, Read, Update, Delete* --- the basic software
operations for storing and editing data. They are necessary engineering
functionality but do not prove the product proposition.

The sub-slice should include at least one knowledge chain such as:

``` text
foundational arithmetic/algebra
        ↓
formula manipulation
        ↓
electrical relationship
        ↓
applied electrical calculation
```

It must demonstrate:

1.  learner answers an electrical question;
2.  system records evidence against relevant assertions;
3.  repeated evidence distinguishes likely root cause;
4.  maths weakness can be identified separately from electrical
    weakness;
5.  learner receives targeted remediation;
6.  learner is tested at the foundational level;
7.  learner is tested again in electrical context;
8.  mastery changes only when supported by evidence;
9.  next activity responds to updated mastery;
10. learner can understand why the system is recommending the activity.

If we cannot make this experience compelling in a small slice, adding
thousands of questions will not fix the product.

# 15. Launch competitive requirements

Before public launch, the Electrical/Maths product should meet the
following standard.

## Knowledge coverage

-   required curriculum coverage is measurable;
-   important assertions have provenance;
-   knowledge is version-aware;
-   proprietary references are acknowledged without unlawful
    reproduction.

## Practice

-   sufficient question diversity to avoid memorisation;
-   realistic qualification-style questions;
-   deterministic marking wherever appropriate;
-   useful explanations;
-   reference-location practice for open-book/reference-based
    assessments.

## Diagnosis

-   concept-level rather than merely unit-level performance;
-   prerequisite weakness detection;
-   misconception tracking where useful;
-   confidence/evidence thresholds before declaring mastery.

## Remediation

-   targeted learning based on diagnosed cause;
-   short focused interventions;
-   progressive difficulty;
-   return-to-context testing.

## Retention

-   spaced retrieval;
-   rechecking previously mastered material;
-   detection of knowledge decay.

## Learner experience

-   clear onboarding;
-   fast mobile experience;
-   useful dashboard;
-   obvious next action;
-   ability to choose Learn / Practice / Target Weaknesses / Quick
    Revision / Mock;
-   learner control rather than opaque automation.

## Trust

-   transparent references/provenance where appropriate;
-   clear distinction between official source and our explanation;
-   no invented authority;
-   grounded AI assistance.

## Security/reliability

As already required by the Playbook:

-   secure authentication;
-   server-side secrets;
-   RLS/data isolation;
-   protected routes;
-   input validation;
-   rate limiting;
-   logging/monitoring;
-   safe error handling;
-   production debug/admin lockdown.

# 17. B2C versus B2B competitive opportunity

**B2C --- business-to-consumer** means selling directly to individual
learners.

**B2B --- business-to-business** means selling to colleges, training
providers or employers.

## B2C opportunity

Electrical appears attractive because learners already independently
purchase:

-   apps;
-   mock exams;
-   books;
-   courses;
-   revision packs.

The initial product can therefore validate without institutional
procurement.

## B2B opportunity

CENTURY demonstrates that FE institutions value:

-   diagnostics;
-   adaptive learning;
-   dashboards;
-   assignments;
-   integrations.

Electrical colleges/training providers could eventually value:

-   cohort weakness dashboards;
-   curriculum coverage;
-   learner readiness;
-   targeted assignments;
-   qualification analytics;
-   OneFile/ePortfolio integration.

This should influence architecture but **must not delay the first
excellent learner product**.

# 18. Strategic risks identified

## Risk 1 --- free is better than expected

Electrical learners can already access good free mocks.

**Response:** compete on learning intelligence, not access to questions.

## Risk 2 --- adaptive learning is not novel

Maths competitors already provide it.

**Response:** prove deeper cross-domain/prerequisite diagnosis.

## Risk 3 --- general AI improves quickly

Learners can already use AI for explanations and question generation.

**Response:** AI is an interface to governed knowledge and learner
state, not the product's source of truth.

## Risk 4 --- incumbents own authoritative content

Awarding bodies and publishers possess official/licensed materials.

**Response:** use lawful independent content with explicit provenance
and superior learning delivery.

## Risk 5 --- platform scope becomes enormous

Trying to beat textbooks, colleges, AI tutors, mock sites and
institutional LMSs simultaneously could prevent launch.

**Response:** first sub-slice proves the unique loop; launch scope
remains disciplined.

# 20. Product Owner decision points

WP0.3 proposes the following decisions for approval:

1.  Treat **Sparky Tests** as the minimum serious electrical practice
    benchmark rather than comparing ourselves only with weak quiz apps.
2.  Treat **CENTURY** as evidence that adaptive maths/weakness
    identification is already established and cannot be claimed as
    unique.
3.  Position Foundational Maths primarily as a **cross-domain
    knowledge/remediation layer**, with standalone Functional Skills as
    an additional product opportunity.
4.  Make **root-cause, cross-domain diagnosis → targeted remediation →
    transfer retest** the defining product hypothesis.
5.  Require the first development sub-slice to prove that loop.
6.  Do not use AI tutor, question count, mocks, progress bars or generic
    personalisation as headline differentiation.
7.  Continue with Electrical as the first full vertical.
8.  Preserve B2B architecture potential, but optimise the initial
    learner experience for direct learner usefulness.
